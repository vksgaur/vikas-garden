The Enlightenment was the great intellectual awakening of 17th and 18th century Europe. It did something no army ever could — it changed how human beings _think_.

Before the Enlightenment, most of life operated under two unquestioned authorities — the Church and the Monarchy. To question these was heresy. Then came thinkers like [[John Locke]], Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu.

## Core Ideas

- **Reason over faith** — truth must be discovered through observation, not received from authority
- **Natural rights** — every person is born with rights that no king can violate
- **Social contract** — governments exist to serve the people, not the other way around
- **Separation of powers** — the key institutional safeguard against tyranny

## Why It Matters

The Enlightenment gave us [[Democracy]], [[Human Rights]], secularism, free press, public education, and the [[Scientific Method]] as a cultural norm. Every major democratic constitution traces its ancestry here.

> Once you assert that all humans possess reason and inherent dignity, you cannot logically confine those rights to one race, one gender, or one class forever.

The Enlightenment was not without contradictions — many of its champions owned slaves. Its "universal" rights were initially for propertied white men. But its own logic became the weapon used to dismantle these hypocrisies.
