The Scientific Method is not a specific technique. It is the **institutionalization of systematic doubt** — the formal acknowledgement that we do not already know everything, and that the best way to learn is to try to prove ourselves wrong.

## The Core Loop

1. **Observe** something
2. **Hypothesize** an explanation
3. **Test** the hypothesis with an experiment designed to _disprove_ it
4. **Publish** your methods so others can replicate and challenge
5. **Revise** based on new evidence

This sounds obvious now. In 1650, it was radical to the point of being dangerous.

## Why It's So Powerful

The Scientific Method produces [[Compounding]] knowledge. Once you build a truth-generating machine, the rate of discovery accelerates non-linearly. Newton builds on Galileo. Darwin builds on geology. Einstein builds on Newton.

> I have seen further because I stood on the shoulders of giants. — Newton

## The Enlightenment Connection

The founding of the Royal Society in London (1660) was the moment Europe built this machine. Its motto: *Nullius in verba* — take nobody's word for it.

Every other intellectual tradition produced brilliant individual thinkers. The [[The Enlightenment|Enlightenment]] produced a _system_ for generating new knowledge. That's the difference that mattered.
