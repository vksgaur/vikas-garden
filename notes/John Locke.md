John Locke (1632–1704) was an English philosopher whose ideas became the intellectual foundation of [[Democracy]] and [[Human Rights]].

## Key Ideas

- **Natural rights** — every person is born with rights to life, liberty, and property. These rights are not granted by any government; they exist prior to government.
- **Social contract** — people form governments to _protect_ their natural rights. Government is a servant, not a master.
- **Right of revolution** — if a government violates the people's trust by infringing their rights, the people are justified in overthrowing it.
- **Tabula rasa** — the mind is a "blank slate" at birth; knowledge comes from experience, not innate ideas.

## Influence

Locke's *Two Treatises of Government* (1689) directly influenced:
- The American Declaration of Independence (Jefferson borrowed "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness" from Locke's "life, liberty, and property")
- The French Revolution's Declaration of the Rights of Man
- Every subsequent democratic constitution

He is arguably the most influential political philosopher in history. His thinking sits at the heart of [[The Enlightenment]].
