The concept of universal human rights is historically recent. For most of human history, the idea that a peasant had the same inherent rights as a king would have been laughed at — or punished.

## Origins

The philosophical foundation was laid by [[John Locke]] and the broader [[The Enlightenment|Enlightenment]] movement. Locke argued for natural rights; the French Revolution codified them in the Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789).

## The Modern Framework

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) — drafted after the horrors of World War II — is essentially an Enlightenment document in international form. It asserts that all humans are "born free and equal in dignity and rights."

## The Paradox

The same civilisation that invented human rights also invented colonialism, slavery, and the Holocaust. The Enlightenment's own logic became the weapon used to fight these contradictions — but the fight is far from over.
