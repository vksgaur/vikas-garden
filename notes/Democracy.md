Democracy is not simply "majority rule." It is a sophisticated system of **distributed power** with institutional safeguards to prevent any single person or group from accumulating too much control.

## Key Components

1. **Free elections** — but these alone don't make a democracy
2. **Rule of law** — laws apply equally to rulers and citizens
3. **Separation of powers** — an idea from Montesquieu, refined during [[The Enlightenment]]
4. **Protection of minority rights** — democracy without this is just mob rule
5. **Free press** — citizens can't hold power accountable without information

## The Enlightenment Connection

Modern democracy is essentially an Enlightenment invention. [[John Locke]] provided the philosophical foundation: governments derive their legitimacy from the consent of the governed. If they violate the people's trust, the people can replace them.

This was radical. Before Locke, kings ruled by divine right. After Locke, they ruled by consent — and consent could be withdrawn.

## Why It Works

Democracy works not because it always produces the best decisions, but because it has the best **error-correction mechanism**. Bad leaders can be removed without violence. Bad policies can be changed at the next election. This is why [[Institutions Matter More Than Geography]] — the ability to peacefully correct course is the most valuable institutional feature a society can have.
