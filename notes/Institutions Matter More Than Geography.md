One of the most important insights from studying [[Why Nations Rise and Fall]] is that **institutions** — the rules, norms, and organizations that structure political and economic life — are the primary determinant of long-term prosperity.

## The Evidence

South Korea and North Korea share identical geography, climate, and ethnic heritage. In 1945, they were the same country. By 2024, South Korea is one of the world's wealthiest nations; North Korea is one of the poorest. The _only_ difference is institutions.

The same pattern repeats everywhere:
- **Nogales, Arizona vs. Nogales, Mexico** — same city, same geography, different institutions, radically different outcomes
- **West Germany vs. East Germany** — one became an economic powerhouse, the other stagnated
- **Botswana vs. most of sub-Saharan Africa** — same continent, but Botswana built inclusive institutions post-independence

## Why This Matters

If geography determined destiny, Singapore — a tiny island with no natural resources — would be poor. It's one of the richest countries on Earth because it built extraordinary institutions.

This insight connects to [[The Enlightenment]] because it was Enlightenment thinking that first articulated the idea that societies could be _designed_ through rational institutional architecture.
