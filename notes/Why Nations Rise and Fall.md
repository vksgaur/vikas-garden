This is perhaps the most important question in the social sciences. And the answer isn't what most people think.

## Common Wrong Answers

- **Geography** — "some places are just better situated." No. [[Institutions Matter More Than Geography]].
- **Culture** — "some cultures are just harder-working." This is circular reasoning that explains nothing.
- **Resources** — "resource-rich countries succeed." Many resource-rich countries (Venezuela, Congo) are desperately poor. This is the "resource curse."

## The Real Answer

Nations rise when they develop **inclusive institutions** — political systems that distribute power broadly, and economic systems that allow people to innovate, compete, and keep the fruits of their labor.

Nations fall when their institutions become **extractive** — when a narrow elite captures political power and uses it to extract wealth from everyone else.

This framework comes from Daron Acemoglu and James Robinson's *Why Nations Fail*, but the intellectual roots go back to [[The Enlightenment]] and thinkers like [[John Locke]] who first argued that legitimate government must protect property and liberty.

## The Compounding Effect

Inclusive institutions create a virtuous cycle: broad power → good policies → prosperity → stronger institutions. Extractive institutions create a vicious cycle: concentrated power → bad policies → poverty → weaker institutions.
